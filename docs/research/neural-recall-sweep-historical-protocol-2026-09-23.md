# Historical recall-floor sweep

The historical arm reuses the original eight exact-label recordings, four source groups, original 18-recording training corpus, six model families, and three seeds. It evaluates every integer calibration recall floor from 90% through 100%. This is a new operating-point comparison; it does not change the old 95% or 99% results.

The frozen NAS protocol is `2026-09-23-recall-sweep-generalization/historical-sweep-v1/protocol.json`, SHA-256 `2cfb767d272d1b46deed54977dbb774f5c64d5c4d9b60b129cfacacbc1ac01e0`. It binds the numerical source closure, original registrations, exact-label revision, source-owner graph, and refit task plan. No protected recording is opened in this arm.

Each source-held outer fold uses its original inner source-held predictions. The four epochs (5, 15, 30, 60) and original 48 ordered decoders form the same 192 candidates. Candidate interval metrics are computed once per fold. At each recall floor, select the highest pooled `F1_padP_coreR` among candidates whose pooled inner `R_core` reaches that floor, preserving original candidate order for ties. An infeasible fold has no fallback. The floor is a calibration constraint, not a promise that held recall reaches the same value.

The primary product padding is two seconds before and after. All results also retain zero-, one-, and three-second sensitivity cases. Model and human export intervals merge touching ranges and join positive gaps strictly below three seconds, then subtract ignored time without rejoining across it. Historical inference preserves the old ignored-segment validity mask for numerical comparability. New deployment and new-panel experiments use a separate, blind full-timeline inference policy; they must not be pooled with this historical inference scope.

All four outer epochs are needed both for the nested sweep and for subsequent original-corpus out-of-fold deployment calibration. There are 72 supplemental outer fits, 202 missing epochs, and 86 already available epochs. Every fit saves all four epochs once. Each available original checkpoint must match the supplemental weights, probabilities, and full overlapping numerical/exposure history exactly. Ten existing distilled outer students and their feature caches are reused; only the two absent outer students are fitted, with the original recipe and complete held-source exclusion.

The independent metric audit recomputes all candidate interval metrics and strict choices, all distinct selected held decodes, all four padding cases, and pooled scope. It verifies that incomplete folds or seeds do not receive a complete result or a favorable-subset mean. A separate refit audit checks every artifact, independently reconstructs source/scaler membership and optimizer exposure, rechecks original numerical parity, and replays first/middle/last real-context held chunks on CPU at every checkpoint. The two new students also receive independent encoder-feature, membership, frozen-BatchNorm, and exposure checks. The original image/teacher audit is reused only after rehashing its evidence.

The reusable engine is `analysis/neural_recall_sweep.py`; saved-fit adapters are in `analysis/neural_recall_sweep_adapters.py`. Its evaluation strata are deliberately separate: exact rallies support core and event metrics; reviewed drafts support approximate live-time overlap; reviewed exports support fixed human-export overlap. Explicit export-rally-proxy calibration is available only through `selection_policy='export-rally-proxy-selection'` and marks its candidate metrics as proxy rather than gold accuracy. The historical arm uses exact labels only.

Qualification before the frozen run: 11 unit tests passed. A synthetic hash-bound bundle independently audited 192 candidate metrics, 11 floor selections, and 33 pooled panel cases across all three evaluation strata. Training uses one GPU process, original four CPU threads for the older heads and two for distilled fits. Code and configuration are fixed; all generated artifacts, temporary files, and caches are on the NAS.

Two resource exceptions were authorized after launch without numerical changes: a brief sequential 32-frame DINO/Mobile wrapper qualification, and a separate composition-study temporal worker on CPU cores 4–5 while this worker uses cores 0–3. The additional worker excludes student encoder training and stops launching new tasks below the recorded free-memory limits. These overlapping runs provide no isolated hardware timing measurements. The independent provenance audit subsequently passed all 216 logical inner views and 144 physical original owners; control replay reproduced all old 95% evaluations and the exact per-recording intervals from the old 99% study.
