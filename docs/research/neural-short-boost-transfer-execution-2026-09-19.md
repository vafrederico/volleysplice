The fixed hypothesis is deliberately simple: double the live BCE contribution of existing supervised positive ticks belonging to an original rally lasting at most three seconds. All other ticks retain multiplier one. This preserves longer-rally positive weights instead of reducing them. Exact and approximate draft supervision use their existing masks; censored draft events remain unknown. Original duration determines membership even if ignored time splits an event. Export-only supervision remains the existing keep-head loss.

Each architecture has three arms: `baseline`, `short_boost`, and `global_control`. For each recording, let P be its number of supervised live-positive ticks and S the subset in original rallies lasting at most three seconds. The short arm assigns multiplier 2 to those S ticks. The global control assigns float32(1 + S/P) to every supervised live-positive tick, or 1 when P=0. Their total positive live-loss mass matches within float32 rounding. This matches a full recording's mass, not each stochastically sampled minibatch. Negative weights, exact-only class weights, unweighted valid-mask denominators, other heads and all sampling remain fixed. The single boost factor and cutoff are not a tuning grid.
